## Defects

Fixed since the review:

- **B33 (refactor, 3 November 2026).** `world()` submitted every leg through one direct backend call by default, modelling a submission shape no real participant can produce: only licensed PSPs reach the settlement layer and each sends only its own customer's payment, so a single request carrying legs from different providers cannot exist. Per-payer provider submission is now the default (`initiation: 'per_payer_psp'`): each payer's `SimulatedPsp` submits only its own legs and every status query and refund goes through the payer's own PSP (`psp.status()`/`psp.refund()`), never `backend.*` directly. Direct initiation survives as two guarded modes — `coordinator_as_psp`, where the coordinator is the licensed provider for every payer of the purchase and a submit whose payers span more than one provider is refused before dispatch (`request_refused { reason: 'direct_initiation_mixed_psps' }`, thrown error, purchase stays `ready`), and `test_bypass_psp`, the same routing with the guard off, explicitly a test fixture; the coordinator option `initiation` is a tagged union whose `undefined` remains the raw unguarded seam for hand-built coordinators. `providerFor` is the fixture's provider-membership data (default `psp-<payerId>`): it groups payers onto shared `SimulatedPsp` adapters — a shared provider gets one adapter and conflicting `supportsMandatePayments` flags on it fail loudly — and feeds the direct-mode guard. Every `world()` trace opens with `initiation_mode { mode }` (actor `world`) so recorded runs say which mode produced them; `FIXTURE_VERSION` is `2026.11-3`. Under per-payer atomic initiation, a send that sees a conclusive backend outcome (`executionRef` plus `settled`/`rejected`) resolves still-`unknown` sibling legs immediately through their own route's `status()`, logged `group_outcome_observed` — same-moment sends stay pending-free while a genuinely unresolved group (lost responses, legs still assembling) stays `pending` for `reconcile()`. S04's distinguishing condition is now payments arriving at different times: the employer PSP is down at the first submit (the employee leg waits `unknown` in the assembling group), recovers, and the refused leg resubmits under its frozen keys before reconcile completes the purchase. All 34 scenario checks pass with outcomes unchanged; trace digests shift by design — new `initiation_mode`, `forward`, `group_assembled` and `group_outcome_observed` events and the `world` actor — and were regenerated.

- **B32 (refactor, 2 November 2026).** Live `Purchase` records crossed the coordinator's public seam: every caller held the mutable record and could write `state`, `proposal`, `legs` or `authorizations` directly; `rec.state` and `rec.proposal` were assigned at many sites so the trace could drift from the record; `openEmployeePurchases` indexed only the employee, leaving split purchases invisible to standing-terms refresh; and a reused `purchaseId` was survived downstream (B12's ledger and `execOwners` defenses) rather than refused at the door. The coordinator now owns `purchases: Map<purchaseId, Purchase>` and the public API takes `purchaseId` throughout, returning ids from `propose`/`proposeSplit` and handing out `view(id)` — a memoized deep read-only proxy (`PurchaseView`) that reflects later mutations and throws on every write shape — while `snapshot()` still returns a detached `Purchase` via `unwrapView`. `commitProposal`, `transition` and `adopt` are the only write paths for `proposal` and `state`, enforced mechanically by a source-scan lint over `coordinator.ts`; every transition including insertion emits a `state` event (the old `cancelled_before_submission` event is now `state { to: 'cancelled', reason: 'cancelled_before_submission' }`), `proposal` events carry `cause` (`proposed`/`split`/`offer_changed`) and the post-commit `state`, and versions start at 1 with no observable `version: 0` skeleton — `reprice` is gone, `changeOffer` delegates. A refused proposal still logs `proposal_rejected` but leaves no record, no index entry and no `proposal` event, and `duplicate_purchase` refuses a reused id before any state or trace change. The reachable-transition table in `src/core/states.ts` throws `illegal_transition` on unreachable moves. The eager `byPayer` index — written only at commit and cleared on non-open transition — covers every payer of every open purchase and backs `openFor(payerId)` (insertion-ordered ids); `inFlight`, `deferredRefresh` and `refreshFailures` rekeyed to ids. `execOwners` and the ledger's `reserve`/`benefitHeld` ownership checks remain: `duplicate_purchase` dedupes only within one coordinator, and two coordinators may still share one backend. Trace digests and event seqs shifted by design (the added `state` events); scenario outcomes are unchanged and `docs/presenter-comparison.md` was regenerated. Explorer follow-up (the artifact lives outside this repo): the spec's §5 items — the Purchase-store card part, a `state`-event trace note, and `cause` in the proposal drawer — stay open for wherever `Multipayer Explorer v10.dc.html` is maintained.

- **B31 (refactor, 2 November 2026).** Verification happened wherever a record was read, so helpers that composed into one decision each paid for their own signature checks: `reprice` verified the employer allowance in `resolveBenefit` and again to name the share, `changeOffer` up to three times; submit verified the standing terms, the retained mandate snapshot carrying the same bytes, and the snapshot again in its evidence loop; a validity projection read the standing record per authorization. The root cause — no way to say "this operation already verified record X" — is fixed by `VerifiedAuthority` (`src/core/verified-authority.ts`): one synchronous decision's view of published authority, created at the start of each operation's uninterrupted stretch and dropped before the first `await`. Registry reads and signature verdicts are memoized per scope, keyed by signed content (holder, key id, signature, canonical payload) rather than object identity, so the registry's in-place fault-injection seams produce fresh keys and still fail closed — a record corrupted between two operations is caught at the next decision, and a removed signing key stays removed because nothing is cached across stretches. Deliberate fresh reads stay fresh: submit's admission, each deferred per-leg first handoff, and the post-rollback deferred refresh are separate decisions with separate scopes. `authorizationCurrentlyValid` gained an optional `record` parameter — the projection's own verified read — so the presenter shares one read across its authorization list (a public-signature addition). Verification counts pinned by `test/verified-authority.test.ts`: auto-pay propose 3→2, offer change 4→2, auto-pay submit 4→2, device-approval submit 4→3, one standing read plus one signature check per authorization in a validity loop. Trace output, event order and presenter digests are unchanged.

- **B30 (refactor, 2 November 2026).** Employee-terms use sites could read the verified standing record more than once per decision: `assessEmployee` read the registry itself while `reprice`, `refreshAuth`, `authorizationCurrentlyValid` and `firstHandoffRefusal` each read it again for expiry noting, refusal checks and mandate evidence. Two reads within one decision could disagree — the assessment and the evidence were not provably the same record. `assessEmployee` now takes the verified record as a parameter; every entry point performs exactly one verified read per decision and passes that same record through all its uses, refusing a record that does not belong to the employee being assessed. `employee_mandate_invalid` still propagates from the caller's read at the same position relative to the blocked check. Submit's per-leg first-handoff recheck on the per-PSP route remains a deliberately fresh read at a later decision point. A read-count regression pins one verified read for propose, offer change, detached assessment, quote, open-state refresh, approval recording, validity projection and whole-request submit.

- **B29 (refactor, 1 November 2026).** Authority publication had two seams: the coordinator audited employee publishes and owned the reference-exclusivity check, while `EmployeeAuthority` was a private coordinator store beside the injected `MandateRegistry`. There is now one registry — `AuthorityRegistry` (`src/core/authority.ts`, the renamed `MandateRegistry`) — that checks every authority record when published and stores both kinds keyed by owner: employer allowances per reference, employee standing terms per employee, each keeping its own version semantics. The reference-namespace exclusivity (`ref_owned_by_employer` / `ref_owned_by_employee`, checked before each kind's own validation) is internal to the registry, which sees both namespaces — the coordinator-side asymmetry is gone. The registry emits `employee_mandate_published`/`employee_mandate_revoked`/`employee_mandate_rejected`/`employer_mandate_rejected` itself with actor `registry` on the shared trace at the coordinator's simulated clock; accepted employer publishes still emit no event. The coordinator no longer publishes or stores authority: `Coordinator.publishEmployerMandate`/`publishEmployeeMandate` are removed, callers publish through `w.authority`; `Coordinator.employeeMandate(id)` stays as a verified read through the registry, and the use-time signature rechecks are unchanged (proposal, approval, submit, runner step — fail closed). Open-purchase refresh on accepted standing terms now runs through `AuthorityRegistry.subscribe()`: the coordinator subscribes and runs the same refresh (deferred behind the operation guard), no polling. Reads renamed: `MandateRegistry.current(ref)` → `AuthorityRegistry.employerMandate(ref)` (live record, the employer fault-injection seam); `EmployeeAuthority.current` is reached as `employeeMandate(employeeId)` (verified detached clone); `storedEmployeeMandate(employeeId)` is the new employee fault-injection seam — a corrupted stored record fails closed at use for both kinds. Exposing the stored employee record made its version field mutable through the seam, so the employee version sequence now also consults private acceptance-time state, matching the employer side: corruption can only fail at use, never reopen the rollback window. All 28 scenario trace digests changed for exactly one reason: the three employee events' actor moved `coordinator` → `registry`.

- **B28 (fixed, 1 November 2026).** `MandateRegistry.publish` stored signed employer mandates unverified: a wrongly signed, tampered or malformed record entered the registry and relied entirely on the coordinator's check at use to fail closed. Publication is now the trust decision, matching `EmployeeAuthority`: `EmployerMandate` gained a `version` field, and `publish` schema-checks the payload, verifies the signature against the key bound to the named employer, keeps each reference bound to its first employer, and requires the reference's next version — so a stale signed revision can never roll back a revocation. Ownership and version decisions consult private acceptance-time state, not the mutable retained record, so corrupting the record through the `current()` fault-injection seam can only deny funding at use — never reopen the rollback window or rebind a reference. Rejected publishes throw `EmployerMandateRejection` through the new audited `Coordinator.publishEmployerMandate` (`employer_mandate_rejected`, LOCAL) and leave the accepted record untouched; the symmetric `ref_owned_by_employee` check keeps employer and employee reference spaces distinct in both directions. The check at use is unchanged and fails closed at proposal, quote, initiation and resubmit. Verification and stored-copy failures (outside-the-signed-domain or uncloneable extra fields) reject as `malformed` rather than escaping untyped, on both registries. All 28 scenario trace digests are unchanged.

## Domain check

- The model applies EUR 7.67 per meal, one subsidised meal per working day (S10). Present it as an illustrative German example until a tax adviser confirms:
  - **Benefit in kind or cash wages:** whether an employer contribution paid directly to the merchant in digital euro qualifies as a benefit in kind (meal-voucher treatment) at all, or counts as cash wages. If it is cash wages, the EUR 7.67 framing does not apply.
  - **Qualifying days:** the per-day conditions (no subsidy on leave, sick or travel days with a per-diem allowance). The model treats every calendar day as a working day.
  - **Taxable benefit:** the employee co-payment rule. The benefit is tax-free only if the employee pays at least the Sachbezugswert. The reference case (EUR 8) passes, but the policy does not check it.
  - **Amounts:** the 2026 values (EUR 4.57 Sachbezugswert + EUR 3.10).
