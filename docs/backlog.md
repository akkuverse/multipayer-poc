# Backlog

Known defects and planned work. Defects B1–B5 from the 14 October 2026 review are fixed; see git history.

## Defects

Open: none.

Fixed since the review:

- **B16 (fixed, 19 October 2026).** During a backend/PSP wait, `submit` left the purchase `ready`. `cancel` could release its benefit reservation, a second lunch could draw that benefit, and the first submission could later complete too. Overlapping mutations could also replace terms or legs and race status/return responses. The coordinator now owns one asynchronous operation at a time per purchase object: `submit`, `resubmit`, `reconcile`, `recover` and `refund` acquire the same guard before work and release it in `finally`; every competing mutation refuses with `operation_in_progress` before effects. Other purchases can still run. Existing method signatures, purchase states and sequential scenario traces are unchanged. Regressions in `test/coordinator-concurrency.test.ts` hold real mock calls before dispatch or response delivery, cover direct/per-PSP initiation and every asynchronous operation, and check reservation protection, independent purchases and release after refusal. This is per-coordinator protection; cross-coordinator ownership and direct caller mutation of live purchases remain outside it.

- **B15 (fixed, 16 October 2026).** UCP search could throw while formatting a malformed JSON `context.currency`; unknown request keys also produced invalid or misdirected JSONPaths. Currency diagnostics now avoid coercing non-strings, and both operations share escaped member-path construction. Lookup also omitted required variant `inputs` correlation, incorrectly described as unavailable in the contact report; each resolved variant now carries its deduplicated request id. Regressions in `test/ucp-catalog.test.ts` cover all three defects at the operation interface.

- **B14 (fixed, 16 October 2026).** Presenter messages cited discovery/quote/explanation events containing only references or empty details, so digest coverage did not substantiate the displayed claims. Session events now retain detached recipient-safe observations and discovery preferences; agent evidence references only those observations. Journey checks validate offer, amount, proposal, status and reason claims against earlier observations. Manual comparison rows now accurately report that no agent handle was used. The generated comparison was refreshed.

- **B13 (fixed).** `Coordinator.benefitNow` checked only `totalCents <= employeeMaxCents` in its missing/invalid-mandate fallback, so an unknown mandate let EUR 8 total + EUR 1 fee pass under an EUR 8 instruction. The shared benefit resolver extracted for `Coordinator.quote` now includes the employee fee in every instruction check; regression for unknown and invalidly signed mandates in `test/defects.test.ts`.

- **B6 (fixed).** `reconcile` on a purchase that was never submitted derived `completed` from a vacuous all-settled check on zero legs — a status query alone could complete a purchase with no money moved. `reconcile` now refuses with `nothing to reconcile` when the purchase has no legs. Found by `test/invariants.property.test.ts` (seed 1); regression in `test/defects.test.ts`. The sibling path — a zero-amount proposal completing vacuously — is also closed: `propose`/`changeOffer` refuse non-positive or non-integer totals.
- **B7 (fixed).** `resubmit` had no state guard and no authority recheck, so a leg refused before reaching the backend (no `executionRef`) got its *first* initiation on retry — it could settle under a revoked mandate and could reopen `failed`, `cancelled` or `recovery_required` purchases after the benefit reservation was released or money was returned. `resubmit` now runs only in `pending` (or `completed`, where the backend dedupes) and re-runs the initiation recheck — approval signature and expiry, or the employer mandate via `benefitNow` — for every leg that was never forwarded. Found by the plan's integrative review; regression in `test/defects.test.ts`.
- **B8 (fixed).** `MockBackend.submit` resolved each leg's accounts inside `settle`, so an unknown payee or fee collector on a later leg threw *after* earlier legs had settled — a thrown request left committed money the coordinator could no longer see. Accounts for every leg in the request are now resolved before the first debit and before any idempotency key is recorded, and again before an assembled group settles (an account may vanish mid-assembly; the group stays alive so its deadline can still resolve it). Found by the integrative review; regression in `test/defects.test.ts`.
- **B9 (fixed).** `derive` had no case for `returned` legs of a `completed` purchase, so a `reconcile` or `resubmit` after a refund relabelled the purchase `cancelled` (full refund) or `recovery_required` (mid-refund bail), after which `refund` itself refused — the same logical state got two labels by call order. Returned legs of a completed purchase now count as settled in `derive`, matching `refund`'s contract that the purchase stays `completed`. Regression in `test/defects.test.ts`.
- **B10 (fixed).** The backend deduplicated on idempotency key alone, so reusing a `purchaseId` minted the same keys and a second purchase deduped into `completed` — paying nothing to its own merchant. A repeated key now must carry identical terms; a mismatch throws `idempotency key reused with different terms`. Regression in `test/defects.test.ts`.
- **B11 (fixed).** With mixed timeout faults, `submitAtomic` picked the first leg's timeout in leg order, so `timeout_then_settle` on an earlier leg silently dropped a later leg's `timeout_then_reject` — the deferred group could settle a leg whose fault contract said it rejects. Any `timeout_then_reject` or rejection reason now rejects the whole group. Regression in `test/defects.test.ts`.
- **B12 (fixed).** A `purchaseId` collision let the second purchase corrupt the benefit ledger: `reserve` adopted the first purchase's entry silently, and every `release`/`consume` keyed by `purchaseId` ran unconditionally — a thrown submission released the victim's consumed or live reservation and the day's benefit could be drawn twice. `reserve` now refuses any existing entry, and a purchase may only consume or release a reservation it made itself (`Purchase.benefitHeld`). The per-PSP send path also swallowed the backend's deterministic dedupe refusal as an unresolvable `unknown`; a new `RequestRefusedError` marks the leg rejected. A partial collision could also dedupe one leg into the first purchase's execution — arming `recover()` against the victim's money — so the coordinator records which purchase owns each `executionRef` and marks a foreign-deduped leg rejected without recording the reference. Found by the integrative review (rounds 6–7); regressions in `test/defects.test.ts`.

## Domain check

- The model applies EUR 7.67 per meal, one subsidised meal per working day (S11). Present it as an illustrative German example until a tax adviser confirms:
  - **Benefit in kind or cash wages:** whether an employer contribution paid directly to the merchant in digital euro qualifies as a benefit in kind (meal-voucher treatment) at all, or counts as cash wages. If it is cash wages, the EUR 7.67 framing does not apply.
  - **Qualifying days:** the per-day conditions (no subsidy on leave, sick or travel days with a per-diem allowance). The model treats every calendar day as a working day.
  - **Taxable benefit:** the employee co-payment rule. The benefit is tax-free only if the employee pays at least the Sachbezugswert. The reference case (EUR 8) passes, but the policy does not check it.
  - **Amounts:** the 2026 values (EUR 4.57 Sachbezugswert + EUR 3.10).

- **Collision-hardening residuals (integrative round 8).** `execOwners` is per-coordinator, so two coordinators on one backend are unprotected against cross-coordinator purchaseId collisions (same boundary as `BenefitLedger` unless shared via `opts.ledger`). A foreign-marked leg keeps any ref it already recorded — only reachable from a backend violating the same-keys-same-executions contract — and a `psp_unavailable` retry overwrites its `rejectReason`. All three are fail-closed; decide whether to tighten.
- **Complete group after a transient resolution failure.** If `resolveAccounts` throws when an assembled group completes (an account deleted mid-assembly), the group stays in `assembling` but nothing re-triggers completion — a dedupe retry returns the recorded outcome and `status` only expires, so the group can only fail `group_incomplete` even if the account is restored within the window. Fail-closed and deadline-bounded; decide whether a retried submission should re-attempt completion.
- **Benefit day across a pending midnight.** A benefit reservation is booked under the day it was made and consumed under that day, but `resubmit`'s recheck evaluates `benefitNow` — and therefore daily usage — on the *current* day. A purchase pending across midnight can be falsely blocked by another purchase's new-day draw (fail-closed, resolves on group expiry) or record consumption on a different day than the recheck examined. Decide whether the recheck should evaluate the reservation's day.
