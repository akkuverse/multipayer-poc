# Field map

Status: **22 October 2026, updated 6 November 2026.** Which party may legitimately see which purchase field, and why. `viewFor` in `src/core/privacy.ts` implements the party columns as projections — currently a subset of what the map permits (for example, the employee's own mandate *reference* is permitted but deliberately withheld; only the observed revision is surfaced). The payer columns are split by the purchase's signed disclosure profile (see [Signed disclosure profiles](#signed-disclosure-profiles) below): a benefit purchase runs under `employer_benefit_v1`, a peer split under `split_bill_v1`, a conditional fund under `public_good_pledge_v1` and an anonymously matched charity purchase under `anonymous_donation_v1`. Tests in `test/privacy.test.ts` pin the per-profile disclosure contract; `test/views.test.ts` retains the shared projection arithmetic. "Settlement" is what the orchestrator's `SettlementIntent` hands to providers: leg terms and routing only — signed authority stays with the coordinator.

Finding: the settlement layer needs no benefit or authority information beyond each leg's authority kind to settle, so benefit conditions stay in the application. The employer never sees the purchase total, the employee's share, the employee's standing terms or automatic budget, or other purchases. A fund contributor never sees other pledges' identities or amounts, and a counter its signed condition does not name is simply absent from its view. The charity buyer never learns the donor identity, allowance reference or attestation; the donor gets no per-purchase view at all — only its own allowance's aggregate accounting.

## Signed disclosure profiles

Every proposal freezes one member of a closed, versioned catalog — `employer_benefit_v1`, `split_bill_v1`, `public_good_pledge_v1` or `anonymous_donation_v1` — resolved from signed sources at admission and held on `Proposal.privacy` for the version's whole life. The suffix is the rules version: an existing profile's disclosure semantics never change in place. `resolveProfile()` in `src/core/privacy.ts` is the only resolution path; `viewFor()` is the only public purchase-projection entry point, and `Coordinator.allowanceReport`/`allowanceReportFor` the only cross-purchase report path. Neither privacy resolution nor a projection ever grants spending authority, reserves money or initiates settlement.

**Who signs the profile.** The employer signs `privacyProfile: 'employer_benefit_v1'` inside its `EmployerMandate`. The split creator signs `privacyProfile: 'split_bill_v1'` plus a `disclosureGroupRef` in the initial split request. Each payer signs the profile again in their `PayerApproval` (split approvals additionally carry the group reference), and the coordinator compares it against the frozen terms both at approval and at authorization revalidation — a mismatch is `terms_mismatch`, never a reinterpretation. The campaign payee signs `public_good_pledge_v1` inside its `open_fund` request and every contributor re-signs it inside their pledge — the pledge also binds the `campaignDigest` over the complete signed campaign table, so no term can be silently substituted. The donor signs `anonymous_donation_v1` inside its `AllowanceMandate`; the charity buyer signs it in the proposal and again in the `PayerApproval`. Pledges replace the approval echo on funds entirely: a fund is authorized by signed pledges, so its contributor view carries no device-approval payload.

**Source precedence and `request_only`.** Resolution works on already signature-verified records, not caller claims: a benefit request's signed profile must equal the verified mandate's own signed profile, and a split request's group reference must resolve to a configured group that covers the actual share payers. When no usable verified mandate exists, the signed restrictive profile alone resolves to conservative `request_only` terms — no employer share is authorized and no sponsor entitlement exists — while a verified mandate carrying an invalid profile is refused outright (`mandate_privacy_profile_invalid`) rather than masked by that fallback. The same conservative pairing exists under `anonymous_donation_v1`: a charity proposal with no live attestation or no positive candidate freezes `{ kind: 'request_only' }` — the signed anonymous-donation request stands, but no allowance is bound and no sponsor entitlement exists — while a *corrupted* retained record (schema, signature, issuer trust or lookup binding) fails closed with a refusal rather than a silent fallback. A fund source is `{ kind: 'campaign', campaignDigest, terms }`: the complete signed campaign table frozen at open, never re-derived. A matched charity source is `{ kind: 'allowance', allowanceRef, version, donorId }` — internal provenance recording which verified revision the match committed to; it is never copied into a recipient view. Selecting a profile, and the disclosure agreement itself, conveys no spending authority: each payer still authorizes only their own shares under the existing authority rules.

An offer change preserves that frozen source. If later authority would add a positive employer contribution to a `request_only` purchase, the coordinator refuses the replacement with `employer_funding_requires_new_purchase` before changing the existing proposal or authorizations. A new purchase must admit the newly available source. Repricing remains available when the employer contribution stays zero. A fund's terms can never be repriced (`changeOffer` refuses fund purchases), and a charity purchase's shares, selected allowance and merchant cannot be repriced or rematched through `changeOffer` either — a changed arrangement needs a fresh purchase and approval.

**The known-group agreement.** `split_bill_v1` peer disclosure exists only under an explicit, existing group disclosure agreement supplied as trusted configuration (`CoordinatorOptions.knownSplitGroups`, default `colleagues-v1` in the fixtures): an existing group whose members may share their identities and principal contribution amounts within that group. It is a separate configuration input — a signed request can select a group but never install or amend one — and registration alone is not that agreement. A group may cover any purchase subset of its members; every distinct payer must belong to the named group *and* hold a registered identity key, or the request is refused before any purchase exists (`split_group_unknown`, `split_participant_unknown`, `split_group_mismatch`).

**Frozen actual audience.** The disclosed audience is frozen as the distinct payer IDs the committed shares name — a projection shows the actual purchase participants, never the group's whole membership, and `viewFor` refuses frozen terms that no longer match the shares. A roster change means a new split proposal and fresh payment authority; repricing a live split stays unsupported.

**Merchant profile-ID exclusion.** The payee view keeps its existing shape and carries no profile ID: naming `employer_benefit_v1` to the merchant would reveal funding composition. No payer identity or funding explanation appears on it either way. On a fund it additionally carries the anonymous funding outcome (phase and `met`); on a charity purchase the donor leg arrives as an anonymous contribution item like any other.

**The fund contributor contract.** A campaign-source purchase projects `FundPayerView`, a distinct allowlisted shape that can never carry the ordinary `totalCents`/`othersCents`/`sharedContributions` fields. Each contributor sees its own signed pledge terms, the detached acceptance acknowledgment, the pledge's `active`/`lapsed` outcome and own-leg settlement status, the complete public campaign table, and only the progress counters the signed condition itself exposes — `minPayers` authorizes the live contributor count, `minTotalCents` the live pledged principal, both authorize both; an unauthorized counter is an absent key, not a zero. Entitlement comes from retained acceptance history: a lapsed contributor keeps read access to its outcome and the permitted counters without reacquiring funding authority. One acknowledged partial inference channel remains: `acceptance.proposalVersion` records the roster version a pledge was recorded under, which bounds the number of earlier acceptances a contributor can infer on a budget-only (`minTotalCents`-only) campaign whose count is otherwise undisclosed — within the correlation surface the spec acknowledges, not a disclosure-contract breach.

**The anonymous-donation contract.** The buyer sees its own remainder share plus the donor share as role and magnitude only (`donor: €5`); identity, allowance reference and attestation never appear. Because the buyer may not legitimately reconstruct the hidden allocation, its `PayerApproval` binds a coordinator-projected opaque `sharesDigest` over the complete share array — the spec's option A: "I countersign the coordinator's commitment to this allocation", not an independently recomputed digest. The unsalted deterministic digest is a binding, not an anonymity guarantee; fixtures use opaque donor identifiers so it does not trivialize to a lookup. Cryptographic independence stays deferred (issue #1). The sponsor facet is refused outright for this profile — `viewFor(..., { facet: 'sponsor' })` never projects a per-purchase donor view. Donor oversight instead runs through `Coordinator.allowanceReport(allowanceRef, recipient)`: only the owning donor's sponsor facet is entitled; reference knowledge, buyer participation or merchant status grant nothing; unknown, corrupt and unentitled references all return the same `{ refused: 'allowance_report_refused' }` with no event and no ownership or balance detail — revocation and expiry do not remove the owner's access to its historical accounting. The report's counts and amounts span all days and distinguish uncertain reserved work from completed consumption; `remainingLifetimeCents` is a budget remainder (`null` when uncapped), capacity accounting — not a deposited balance and not a promise of daily headroom.

**Donor shortfall vocabulary.** When the fixed donor arrangement no longer verifies at admission or an unstarted first handoff — revoked or expired allowance or attestation, changed terms, exhausted capacity, missing or stale buyer consent — the refusal carries `donor_authority_invalid:<reason>`, the spec's reason vocabulary (`terms_mismatch`, `allowance_revoked`, `attestation_expired`, `cap_exceeded`, `buyer_consent_missing`, and the verified-read problems such as `allowance_signature_invalid` or `attestor_not_trusted`). The gap never migrates to the buyer. A genuine no-match at proposal time is not a refusal: it is recorded as evidence on the purchase (`no_live_attestation`, `no_eligible_allowance` or the allowance's own ineligibility reason) under `request_only` terms.

**Correlation and PSP caveats.** These profiles are participant data minimization, not resistance to all correlation: the coordinator always knows the roster and the donor↔buyer pairing; polling aggregate report differences, sparse campaigns, disclosed amounts, external observation and the deterministic digest can still reveal information. Providers keep their existing visibility — `payerId` per leg, pseudonyms on the digital-euro ledger — and the charity payee's PSP still observes the individual credits it receives. Every fixture identity, attestation and allowance is synthetic; no real beneficiary data exists here.

**Identity enrollment assumption.** Recipient identity is trusted caller context from the session/fixture — a party ID parameter is not a login mechanism; this local PoC still assumes identity and key enrollment. A party holding several legitimate roles requests each facet separately; no facet unions another's data.

**Agent restrictions unchanged.** The agent handle still holds only `discover`, `evaluate`, `selectOffer` and `explain`; it never selects a disclosure profile (presenter benefit requests carry the fixed `employer_benefit_v1`), and no group membership, raw mandate or spending capability reaches the agent interface. The new surfaces are no exception: agents cannot sign `open_fund`, `pledge` or charity proposals, approve or submit — `open_fund` accepts only the payee's key and a charity proposal only the buyer's. `Coordinator.view()` and trusted evaluator snapshots remain raw internal surfaces — evaluator evidence, never offered to agents or represented as privacy-filtered reads.

## Presenter projections

The scripted synthetic presenter (`src/presenter/session.ts`) adds a further-reduced projection for the employee-side recipient and the agent handle. These fields are *safe eligibility results* — the outcome of policy and assessment evaluation — not the underlying policy, mandate or usage data behind them.

| Field | Content | Agent handle | Employee recipient |
|---|---|---|---|
| OfferView | Sourced catalog fields (offer reference, title, tags, price, availability, source, walking time) plus retrieval time | yes | yes |
| QuoteView amounts | Deterministic split from `Coordinator.quote`: own share, combined other contributions, own fee | yes | yes |
| QuoteView assessment | Current `authZone` and `autoPayBlockedReason`, the standing maximum (absent means never published — never an infinite limit), the observed mandate version | yes | yes |
| reasons | Safe eligibility result, allowlist only: `benefit_unavailable`, `merchant_ineligible`, `merchant_blocked`, `personal_limit_exceeded`, `offer_expired`, `approval_expired` | yes | yes |
| PresenterView | Payer view plus selected offer, proposal version/expiry, observed assessment and recorded authorizations | yes | yes |
| RecordedAuthorizationView | Basis (`device`, `exception`, `mandate`), the employee's own approvalRef for signed bases or the cited standing-terms revision for mandate basis, proposal version, whether it binds the current proposal and is currently valid — never the signature or key | yes | yes |
| EmployeeTermsView | The employee's own standing terms: limits, merchant lists, expiry, revocation, revision — never the mandate reference, signature or key | no — trusted user flow only | yes |
| eventSeqs | Observation references into the audit trace; they grant no access to the raw records behind them | yes | yes |

Session observation events retain detached copies of the safe projections, with the assessment and, for discovery, the applied preferences. Agent results reference only these observation events. Trusted user-flow results may additionally reference coordinator/settlement audit events for evaluator use. The trusted `snapshot()` export remains a detached raw purchase for run recording; it is never exposed as an agent observation. A third trusted handle, `runner`, advances the session purchase deterministically; it is never offered as an agent tool.

Never surfaced through recipient projections: mandate references (employee or employer), signatures and keys, raw ledger usage, operation keys or unfiltered settlement errors. The presenter only runs `employer_benefit_v1` purchases, so other payers' identities stay hidden here too; a known-group split discloses the actual participants only to the split's own payers as the profile rules above describe. Neither handle exposes the coordinator, the live purchase or the raw trace. The employee's standing terms and monthly automatic usage are visible to the employee's own surfaces only — the employer's minimisation above is unchanged: it still sees its own contribution and eligibility evidence, never the purchase total, the employee's share or other purchases.
